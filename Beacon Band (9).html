Your friends feel it when you don't feel safe.

A customizable band that reads your sweat chemistry, heart rate, breathing and movement, learns what normal looks like for you, and quietly buzzes the friends and family you chose when you are truly in distress. Your data stays on your wrist.

How it works

Three ideas. That is the whole product.

01

It reads your body and learns you

The band measures sweat chemistry and combines it with heart rate, breathing and movement. An AI model keeps learning your normal, so a hard workout and real fear look different.

02

Your circle feels it

When the band is confident you are in distress, it asks you first, then buzzes the friends and family you chose. Bands in a circle, say four of you, share one simple alert level and pass it along even without phone signal.

03

Your data stays yours

Everything the band records is stored on the band, and only you can open it. If you choose to involve law enforcement, pressing the button sends one thing: the signal that you need help. The evidence stays with you.

The network

One band protects you. A circle of bands protects each other.

Each band learns its own wearer. Together they form a small private network that finds its members, shares only simple signals, and relays an alert when one phone is out of reach.

Signal lab

Four body signals in, one guess about what you are doing

The band does not trust one number. It looks at how heart rate, sweat, breathing and body shakes share the picture, and matches that mix to patterns it has learned for you. Move the sliders. Try 10 / 20 / 50 / 20, then swap breathing and shakes.

5. How it gets better at knowing Ava

Each day the band collects labeled moments: Ava taps "I'm OK", a friend confirms a call, an alert is cancelled. It nudges the matching pattern toward every label. Drag through two weeks.

Beacon Band is a placeholder name for a concept demo. People, readings, dates and recordings shown are sample data, not real. A real product would need clinical testing, consent from everyone in a circle, and compliance with local recording and emergency-services rules. The network features (private circle links, anonymous hellos, relaying alerts without signal) are an idea being explored and have not been built or tested. The signal lab uses invented readings. Its learning is a small working model running in your browser, but whether wrist signals can reliably separate states like running, dancing and fear has not been tested. The optional spray layer would only be offered where pepper spray is legal, and it never releases automatically.
